QPLANT Helium System Architecture

Primary Mode: Liquid Helium at 4K

The QPLANT cryogenic system operates with liquid helium at 4K as the primary coolant. The Warm Storage Holders (WSH) contain the liquid helium inventory. Leaks from the system cause liquid inventory loss through boil-off.

HP Compressor Supply

The HP compressor system provides helium at 14 barg (15 bara) to the main beam cooling loop. Redundancy options range from N=3 (baseline) to N+1 configurations with FSD575 VFD or HSD Twin Combi.

Helium Phase Diagram & Superfluid Transition

Unique Properties of He-4

  • Helium is the only element that remains liquid at 0 K (at ambient pressure)
  • Lambda transition at 2.1768 K: He-I (normal) → He-II (superfluid)
  • Superfluid He-II has zero viscosity and extremely high thermal conductivity
  • Critical point at only 5.195 K, 2.275 bar — very narrow liquid range

Leak Rate to Liquid Inventory Loss — Physics

Mass Conservation Principle

A gas leak from the ullage space above liquid helium causes the liquid to boil off to replace the lost gas molecules. At steady state:

ṁ_leak,gas = ṁ_boiloff,liquid (mass conservation) Gas mass flow from leak rate: ṁ = (Q × M) / (R × T) where: Q = leak rate [Pa·m³/s] (1 mbar·L/s = 0.1 Pa·m³/s) M = 0.004003 kg/mol (He-4 molar mass) R = 8.3145 J/(mol·K) (universal gas constant) T = temperature at leak [K] Liquid volume loss rate: V̇_liq = ṁ / ρ_liq (ρ_liq = 124.96 kg/m³ at NBP)

Key insight: Cold leaks (4K) lose more mass per mbar·L/s than warm leaks (300K), because n ∝ PV/T — more moles at lower T for same throughput.

Liquid He Inventory Depletion — WSH 5,000 L Vessel

Even at the least tight specification (10⁻⁴ mbar·L/s), liquid helium loss from valve leaks is extremely small compared to the 5,000 L inventory. The dominant He loss mechanism in practice is static heat leak (boil-off from insulation imperfection), not valve leaks.

Conclusion: The valve leak rate specification has minimal impact on liquid inventory management. The derogation request for warm valves (higher leak rate) is justified from an inventory perspective.

VFD Energy Savings — FSD575 (400 kW)

VFD provides significant energy savings at partial loads (affinity law: P ∝ Speed³). At typical 70% average load, VFD saves ~57% energy per compressor. At 100% load, VFD introduces 3% loss (power electronics overhead).

Annual savings at 70% avg load (per compressor): ΔE = (328 − 141) kW × 8,000 h = 1,496,000 kWh/yr Cost: 1,496,000 × €0.15 = €224k/yr per unit

Redundancy Cost-Benefit Analysis

Bubble size represents annual energy cost. The HSD Twin Combi offers the best availability per euro of capital investment, while the FSD575 VFD options offer superior energy efficiency through variable-speed operation.

Design Principle

WCS.HP (Worst Case Supply — High Pressure) ensures sidestream activities (recovery, purification, research taps) do not compromise the main 14 barg supply. Fail-safe isolation valves on sidestreams close automatically when HP header pressure drops.

Leak Rate Budget Allocation

Total system budget: 1×10⁻⁵ mbar·L/s (RTM-048 system cap)

CircuitBudget (mbar·L/s)FractionJustification
Main Hp Supply7e-0670%Critical path: compressors → QVB, largest valve count
Sidestreams2e-0620%Non-critical: recovery, purification, research taps (can be shut off)
Contingency1e-0610%Future expansions, measurement error margin
TOTAL1e-05100%RTM-048 system cap

Protection Strategy

  1. Normal: All circuits active, total leak within budget
  2. WCS trigger: HP pressure < 13.5 barg → close sidestream valves, recover 20% budget
  3. Emergency: HP pressure < 13.0 barg → alarm, start backup compressor, reduce beam

WCS.HP Supply Architecture — Block Diagram

Compressors in parallel feed the HP header at 14 barg. Check valves prevent backflow. The main supply branch (QVB/WSH/Beam) receives 70% of the leak budget. Sidestream branches have normally-closed isolation valves that close on low HP pressure.

Warm Valve Leak Tightness Derogation

Background (from Cryoworld Offer)

For warm On/Off valves (W1d), Cryoworld proposes pneumatic ball valves of Meca Inox with HDPE sealing. These valves do not comply with the specified He leak rate of 1×10⁻⁹ mbar·L/s (considered unnecessarily stringent for warm service).

Proposed Approach

  • Create a new sub-class (W1d) with higher allowable leak rate to ambient
  • Ask GBO to perform short calculation on acceptable leak rate

At Warm Temperature (300K)

At 300K, the mass flow for same throughput is reduced by 300/4.222 ≈ 71× compared to 4K. For 1×10⁻⁴ mbar·L/s at 300K: mass loss = 0.51 g/yr per valve. Completely negligible.

ACCEPT The derogation is justified. Even at 1×10⁻⁴ mbar·L/s, the liquid He inventory impact from warm valves is negligible. The economic solution (Meca Inox / Swagelok) is appropriate.

Calculation Methodology Summary

Liquid He Loss (src/liquid_he_loss.py)

ṁ = Q_Pa_m3_s × M_He / (R × T) Q_Pa_m3_s = Q_mbar_l_s × 0.1 V̇_liq = ṁ / ρ_liq (125 kg/m³)

Compressor Reliability (src/compressor_reliability.py)

A_single = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR) A_sys(k-of-M) = Σ_{i=k}^{M} C(M,i) × A^i × (1-A)^(M-i) MTBF_sys ≈ A_sys × MTTR / (1-A_sys)

VFD Savings

P_VFD = P_full × (load)³ / η_VFD (affinity law) P_fixed ≈ P_full × (0.4 + 0.6 × load) (slide valve) ΔCost = ΔP × hours × €/kWh

Conclusions & Recommendations

Liquid Helium Operations

  • Valve leak rates (10⁻⁹ to 10⁻⁴ mbar·L/s) have negligible impact on liquid He inventory
  • The warm valve derogation (1×10⁻⁹ → 1×10⁻⁵ or 10⁻⁴) is justified
  • Static heat leak (insulation quality) dominates boil-off, not valve leaks

HP Compressor Redundancy

  • All options (N=3, N+1 FSD575, N+1 HSD) exceed 99.99% availability
  • Recommended: N+1 FSD575 VFD (2-of-4) for best combination of availability, energy efficiency, and proven technology
  • VFD provides ~€224k/yr energy savings per compressor at 70% load

WCS.HP Protection

  • 70/20/10 leak budget allocation (main/sidestream/contingency) provides adequate margin
  • Interlock logic ensures sidestream isolation before main supply is compromised
  • System can maintain 100% beam at single compressor failure, 50% at double failure
