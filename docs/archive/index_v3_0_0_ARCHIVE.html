FAT Procedure — Valve Leak Testing (EN 13185 + ISO 5208)

1. Preparation

  1. Install valve in test fixture with known volume calibration
  2. Verify helium mass spectrometer calibration (standard leak ±10%, cert <1 year old, NIST-traceable)
  3. Evacuate test volume to <1×10⁻³ mbar
  4. Record ambient temperature (±2°C stability required)
  5. Document valve serial number, manufacturer, model, DN, PN

2. Test Execution

  1. Pressurize upstream side with helium to operating pressure (specified in ITP)
  2. Wait stabilisation period (minimum 5 minutes for thermal equilibrium)
  3. Detect helium on downstream (vacuum) side with mass spectrometer
  4. Record leak rate in mbar·l/s — minimum 60 seconds continuous measurement
  5. Repeat measurement 3 times, report average

3. Acceptance Criteria (RTM Table 6)

TypeLimit (mbar·l/s)Standard
Leak to vacuum (process boundary)<1×10⁻⁸EN 13185 §6.2
Leak to ambient (external)<1×10⁻⁹EN 13185 §6.2
Leak across valve seat (internal)<1×10⁻⁴ISO 5208 §6.3.2
System total<1×10⁻⁵RTM-048

4. Deliverables

  • Leak test data sheet (valve S/N, date, operator, 3× measurements, average, pass/fail)
  • Calibration certificate for mass spectrometer + standard leak
  • Photo evidence of test setup
  • Accept/Reject decision signed by QA inspector

SAT Procedure — System-Level Leak Test

Scope

Entire QPLANT cryogenic helium system after installation — validate RTM-048 and RTM-053.

Method: Pressure Hold Test (24-hour)

  1. Isolate system section under test
  2. Pressurize with helium to operating pressure
  3. Record initial pressure (P₁) and temperature (T₁)
  4. Monitor pressure and temperature continuously for 24 hours (minimum)
  5. Record final pressure (P₂) and temperature (T₂)
  6. Calculate leak rate: Q = (ΔP × V) / Δt, corrected for temperature variation
Q_leak = (P₁ - P₂) × V / Δt    [mbar·l/s]
Temperature correction: Q_corr = Q_leak × (T₁/T₂)

Acceptance

ParameterRequirementSource
Total system leak rate<1×10⁻⁵ mbar·l/sRTM-048

  • Temperature log (to exclude thermal drift effects)

  • Punch list of items requiring re-test (if any)

Full 5-order-of-magnitude leak rate range showing helium mass loss at different temperatures.

Reference lines mark RTM-047 (ambient boundary), RTM-048 (system cap), and Table 6 (seat leakage).

Temperature Effect with Pressure Isolines

Lower temperatures dramatically increase helium loss per unit leak rate due to higher gas density.

Internal vs External Leakage Comparison

Internal (seat) leakage at 10⁻⁴ mbar·l/s produces ~100,000× more mass loss than external (ambient) leakage at 10⁻⁹ mbar·l/s.

Choked Flow Regime Map

Critical pressure ratio for He (γ=1.66): P_down/P_up < 0.4881

At pressure ratios below the critical value, flow becomes sonic and leak rate no longer increases with differential pressure.

Helium Purity Dilution Over Time

Air ingress through leak paths dilutes helium purity. At 10⁻⁴ mbar·l/s total ingress, purity drops measurably within months.

Helium Thermophysical Properties Database

Properties at 18 evaluation points (6 temperatures × 3 pressures). Source: NIST REFPROP correlations for He-4.

Helium Compressibility Factor

At high temperatures (≥50K) and moderate pressures, helium behaves very close to an ideal gas (Z≈1). At 4K and 12 bar (supercritical), Z drops to ~0.62 — significant deviation requiring real-gas corrections.
